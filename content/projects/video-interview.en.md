# Video Interview

## Summary

I participated in designing Video Interview, a feature that emerged from Remote Latinos clients' need to incorporate video interviews into the recruitment process.

The proposal allows candidates to answer a sequence of role-specific questions by recording their responses, with transcription included to support subsequent review.

The goal was to create a clear, organized experience that guides candidates through the interview and accounts for the different situations that can arise when recording a response.

## Role and team

My contribution focused on the feature's UX/UI design, from structuring the candidate journey to defining interactions and the different recording states.

I worked on question organization, the video response flow, and error, retry, and recovery scenarios.

The initial need was raised by Remote Latinos clients, and my work involved translating it into a digital experience proposal suited to a guided interview.

## Context and challenge

Remote Latinos needed a feature that allowed candidates to answer role-specific questions through recorded video interviews.

Unlike a traditional form, this type of experience requires guiding users through a sequential activity involving the camera, recording, and managing their responses.

The main challenge was to design a journey that allowed candidates to focus on each question, understand where they were in the interview, and clearly recognize recording states.

It was also important to account for situations in which the process did not progress as expected, offering alternatives to continue or retry an action.

## Design process

### 1. Defining the journey

The process began by organizing the interview experience around a sequence of questions related to the candidate's role.

The proposal was structured as a guided journey, presenting each question individually to maintain focus on the current response.

### 2. Designing the recording interaction

I worked on the video response experience, defining how to present questions and communicate the different stages of recording.

The design aimed to help candidates clearly identify the action they needed to take and the status of their response.

### 3. States and alternative scenarios

Alongside the main journey, different recording states and edge cases were considered.

Error and retry scenarios were designed to address situations in which candidates needed to recover an action or record a response again.

This work extended the feature beyond the ideal flow, incorporating behavior for alternative situations.

### 4. Integrating transcription

The proposal also included response transcription as part of the interview experience.

This feature was intended to support review of the recorded content by providing a text representation of candidates' responses.

## Key design decisions

- **Step-by-step experience:** present one question at a time to help candidates concentrate.
- **Contextualized questions:** relate the interview content to the role the candidate is applying for.
- **Recording clarity:** visually distinguish interaction states so users understand what is happening.
- **Error handling:** account for alternative scenarios that allow recovery from unexpected situations.
- **Retry options:** incorporate actions that help candidates continue when a recording does not complete correctly.
- **Response transcription:** integrate text content as a complement to the video interview.
- **Interaction consistency:** maintain a recognizable structure throughout the different stages of the journey.

## Solution

A UX/UI proposal was developed for a video interview experience organized around three main components.

### Guided interview

A sequential journey in which candidates answer questions related to the position they are applying for.

Presenting questions individually aims to make each stage easier to understand and keep the interview experience organized.

### Recording and recovery

An interface that accounts for different recording states, including error and retry situations.

The proposal incorporates interactions to communicate task status and offer alternatives when an action does not complete as expected.

### Interview transcription

The experience includes transcription of recorded responses, providing an additional way to consult the interview content.

Together, the proposal aims to connect response preparation, recording, and subsequent review within a single feature.

## Outcomes and learnings

The project produced the UX/UI design of a video interview feature, covering both the main journey and different interaction scenarios.

One of the main contributions was structuring a guided experience that organizes questions and communicates recording states, including situations in which candidates need to retry an action.

The process reinforced the importance of designing experiences that account for errors, interruptions, and ways to continue, alongside the ideal journey.

It also strengthened my ability to translate clients' functional needs into flows and interactions focused on the user experience.

## Next steps

As a next step, it would be valuable to conduct usability testing with candidates to evaluate their understanding of instructions, the clarity of recording states, and the ease of completing an interview.

It would also be important to validate scenarios involving camera permissions, interruptions, response duration, and recording recovery.

These evaluations would help identify opportunities for improvement and refine the experience before further iterations.

## Closing

This project allowed me to explore the design of a feature that combines video interaction, guided content, and different system states.

The main learning was that a good recording experience depends on guiding users throughout the journey and providing clarity when something does not happen as expected, as well as making responses easy to record.
