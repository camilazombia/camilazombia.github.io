# The Bar Colombia: luxury ecommerce

## Summary

I participated in the comprehensive redesign of The Bar Colombia's ecommerce store, aiming to create a shopping experience that reflected the premium character of its products and brands.

The project combined quantitative and qualitative research, luxury market analysis, user profile definition, and the design of different shopping journeys, balancing a sophisticated aesthetic with clear, functional navigation.

## Role and team

My contribution covered UX research, reference analysis, user profile definition, information architecture, and the design of the ecommerce pages.

I also evaluated features using the Kano model and monitored the experience afterward through heatmaps, session recordings, and Google Analytics, identifying opportunities for adjustments and iterations.

The project was part of my work at BlackSip.

## Context and challenge

The Bar aimed to position itself as a leading platform for purchasing premium spirits, offering a digital experience aligned with its brands' values of exclusivity and quality.

The main challenge was to convey this sense of luxury throughout the shopping journey, from product discovery to checkout.

The experience needed to combine a carefully crafted visual presentation with clear information, intuitive navigation, and features that met the expectations of different buyer profiles.

## Design process

The process was structured around Jesse James Garrett's five elements of UX: strategy, scope, structure, skeleton, and surface.

### 1. Research and analysis

I conducted desk research and benchmarking of luxury ecommerce stores and spirits brands, identifying navigation patterns, product presentation approaches, and opportunities for differentiation.

The research included a survey of **144 users**, with questions about shopping habits, preferences, perceptions of luxury, and expected features.

The results, together with Google Analytics data and other references, informed the definition of two user profiles and their shopping journeys.

### 2. Architecture and features

An open card sorting exercise with **6 participants and 30 cards** explored how people grouped products, categories, benefits, and services.

The findings guided the proposed information architecture and catalog organization.

A Kano analysis was also used to evaluate features such as gift packaging, consultations with mixologists, product comparisons, exclusive experiences, and rewards programs.

### 3. Experience design

The research findings informed design pillars focused on exclusivity, visual identity, attention to detail, and ease of purchase.

These criteria guided the structure and design of the ecommerce pages, aiming to maintain a consistent experience from the homepage through the purchasing process.

### 4. Monitoring and iteration

After the design phase, the experience was monitored through heatmaps, session recordings, and Google Analytics.

These tools helped observe user behavior, identify opportunities for improvement, and guide subsequent adjustments to the experience.

## Key design decisions

- **Luxury and functionality:** create a sophisticated experience while preserving clarity and ease of navigation.
- **User-centered architecture:** organize categories, products, and services based on card sorting findings.
- **Product information:** prioritize images, prices, availability, and relevant details to support purchasing decisions.
- **A distinctive experience:** consider benefits, personalization, and valuable content as part of the premium offering.
- **Shopping journey:** maintain consistency across navigation, product exploration, the cart, and checkout.
- **Continuous improvement:** use behavior analysis tools to guide adjustments and iterations.

## Solution

The ecommerce pages were redesigned, bringing together a new visual direction, information architecture, and design criteria focused on a premium experience.

The solution covered the homepage, navigation, categories, product pages, cart, checkout, and other touchpoints in the shopping journey.

The design aimed to express exclusivity through the aesthetic, content organization, product presentation, and ease of interaction.

## Outcomes and learnings

The project produced a comprehensive redesign supported by user research, feature analysis, and information architecture shaped around users' needs.

The participation of 144 survey respondents and 6 card sorting participants provided insights to support design decisions and understand different shopping expectations.

Subsequent monitoring helped identify further opportunities for improvement and iterate on the experience.

One of the main learnings was that a luxury experience depends on clarity, attention to detail, and the quality of each interaction as well as its visual appearance.

## Next steps

As a next step, it would be valuable to conduct further usability testing of the shopping journeys and complement monitoring with conversion, abandonment, and device-specific behavior metrics.

This would help assess the impact of the implemented decisions and continue evolving the experience based on usage data.

## Closing

This project allowed me to connect UX research, strategy, information architecture, and visual design to approach an ecommerce experience from a comprehensive perspective.

The main challenge was to find a balance between exclusivity and usability, recognizing that a premium experience is also built through clear, functional, and carefully designed interactions.
