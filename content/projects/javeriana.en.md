# Academic landing pages

## Summary

I took part in redesigning academic landing pages for undergraduate and graduate programs at Javeriana University, aiming to create clearer, more intuitive experiences tailored to the needs of each audience.

The project combined heuristic evaluation, user interviews, reference analysis, and workshops with faculties to define content, organize sections, and develop visual proposals in Figma.

## Role and team

My contribution included user research, analysis of existing experiences, identification of opportunities for improvement, interface design, and the creation of components and mobile adaptations.

I worked with the marketing lead and faculty representatives, who helped define and review the content and design proposals.

I also supported the design of other university digital experiences, including the help center, blog, event pages, and different sections of the institutional web ecosystem.

## Context and challenge

The main challenge was to address each faculty's specific needs while maintaining visual and functional consistency across the academic pages.

Understanding the different users was also essential. For undergraduate programs, for example, the experience needed to consider both students exploring their academic options and parents involved in the decision, assessing aspects such as benefits, financing, and institutional information.

The proposal aimed to make relevant information easier to access and create clearer journeys for each type of user.

## Design process

The process began with a heuristic analysis of the existing pages, with findings presented to the marketing lead. This evaluation identified opportunities to improve navigation, content organization, and the user experience.

The research included interviews with students and parents who had experience with Javeriana University, as well as people interested in studying there or who had consulted information on the existing landing pages. These conversations revealed different perspectives on searching for academic programs and users' information needs.

Workshops were then held with the faculties to identify needs, missing information, and priority topics. Through collaboration with their leaders, the content and sections of each landing page were defined.

References from other universities were also analyzed to inform design decisions.

Using these inputs, I developed interface proposals that were presented to and refined with the teams involved. Finally, the designs were organized in Figma with components and mobile versions to support implementation.

## Key design decisions

- **User research:** incorporate the perspectives of students, parents, and prospective students to guide the proposals.
- **Audience-specific experiences:** adapt content organization to the needs of each user group and academic program.
- **Collaborative design:** involve faculty leaders in defining content and reviewing proposals.
- **Information hierarchy:** organize sections to make academic programs easier to explore and understand.
- **Visual consistency:** develop reusable components to maintain consistency across pages.
- **Responsive design:** include mobile versions in the development handoff.
- **Integrated experience:** connect the help center to the undergraduate landing pages through consistent interfaces and navigation.

## Solution

Six undergraduate and three graduate landing pages were developed, covering different study formats and program types, including specializations, doctoral programs, and MBAs.

The work also covered the help center associated with the undergraduate journey, designing 20 sections and reorganizing categories and questions to make information easier to find. The proposal aimed to make this space feel like a natural extension of the landing pages, both visually and functionally.

I also contributed to other initiatives within the university's digital ecosystem:

- Support for the institutional blog redesign.
- Modal and landing page design for events.
- A post-registration Thank You Page.
- A new proposal for the university's main homepage.
- A landing page for PIES (Programa Integral de Experiencias Significativas).
- A homepage for prospective students.

## Outcomes and learnings

The project produced design proposals for different academic programs, supported by reusable components and mobile adaptations prepared for development.

The proposals were positively received by faculty teams and earned favorable feedback from some students.

User interviews reinforced the importance of considering different perspectives during the design process, especially when choosing an academic program involves both students and their families.

One of the main learnings was the value of combining user research, UX/UI analysis, and collaboration with academic teams to build consistent experiences adapted to different needs.

## Next steps

As a next step, it would be valuable to complement the interviews with usability testing of the design proposals, evaluating content comprehension, navigation, and ease of finding information.

Interaction and conversion metrics could also be analyzed to identify further opportunities for improvement and continue optimizing the digital experiences.
