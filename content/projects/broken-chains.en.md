# Broken Chains: sneaker ecommerce

## Summary

I led the UX/UI design process for Broken Chains' ecommerce store, aiming to create a shopping experience aligned with the store's identity and the characteristics of the exclusive sneaker market.

The project combined benchmarking, collaboration with the client, and the design of different navigation, shopping, and launch journeys, considering both the regular catalog and limited-availability products.

## Role and team

I was the project's sole UX/UI designer, responsible for the design process from reference research through interface proposals.

I worked directly with the client in collaborative sessions to understand business needs, review proposals, and define how to integrate product launch dynamics into the ecommerce experience.

The project was part of my work at BlackSip.

## Context and challenge

Broken Chains needed a digital experience that represented its identity as a store specializing in exclusive sneakers and addressed the specific demands of its product launches.

The main challenge was to integrate two shopping modes: everyday catalog browsing and drops, where availability, timing, and access conditions play an important role.

The proposal needed to support product discovery, brand and category exploration, launch information, and editorial content within a consistent experience.

## Design process

The process began with benchmarking sneaker ecommerce stores and retailers with similar launch dynamics, analyzing visual references, navigation, product presentation, and shopping experiences.

I then worked directly with the client to understand commercial needs, drop conditions, and opportunities to integrate launches into the digital journey.

Using these inputs, I developed UX/UI proposals for the different ecommerce pages and states, covering both traditional shopping and specific launch scenarios.

The design also included organizing editorial content, search results, filters, and product page variations to address different browsing and availability situations.

## Key design decisions

- **Launch experience:** integrate drops as a central part of the store, with dedicated spaces for upcoming launches and recently released products.
- **Flexible homepage:** design variations for scenarios with no launches, one launch, or up to three simultaneous launches.
- **Adaptable product pages:** distinguish regular product pages from launch pages, incorporating states such as countdowns and subscription options depending on availability.
- **Catalog exploration:** organize navigation by gender, brand, and category, with specific filters to make searching easier.
- **Search and navigation:** include suggestions as users type and no-results states.
- **Editorial content:** integrate articles, events, and launch content into the store experience.

## Solution

The UX/UI redesign proposal covered the main ecommerce pages and their different states.

### Shopping and catalog experience

- Homepage variations for zero, one, and up to three simultaneous launches.
- Men's and women's department pages.
- Brand, category, and search results pages with filters.
- Search with results and suggestions as users type.
- A no-results state for specific searches.
- Mini cart.
- A standard product page.

### Launch experience

- A landing page for upcoming and recent launches.
- Launch product pages with countdowns.
- Product variations with a subscription option depending on availability.
- Information on drop access mechanisms, such as Priority Pass and purchase conditions in physical stores.

### Editorial and institutional content

- Editorial homepage.
- Editorial detail page.
- Launch editorial content.
- Event editorial content.
- Six editorial page variations.
- About Us page.
- Contact page.

The proposal aimed to connect shopping, launches, and brand content within a single experience, maintaining consistent navigation across sections.

## Outcomes and learnings

The project produced a redesign proposal covering different shopping journeys, product states, and launch scenarios.

One of the main contributions was designing a flexible structure that could support both everyday catalog browsing and the specific conditions of drops.

The experience reinforced the importance of understanding the commercial dynamics of a specialized ecommerce store and translating them into interface decisions that connect content, products, and the shopping experience.

It also strengthened my ability to manage a UX/UI process independently and work directly with the client to define solutions.

## Next steps

As a next step, it would be valuable to conduct usability testing with sneaker buyers to evaluate navigation, understanding of launch conditions, and ease of finding products.

User behavior during drops could also be analyzed, particularly on pages with countdowns, subscriptions, and limited-availability products.

These findings would help refine the experience and prioritize future improvements.

## Closing

This project allowed me to approach ecommerce design through the specific needs of a community and its shopping dynamics.

The main learning was that a specialized store needs more than an attractive catalog: it needs an experience that naturally connects product discovery, anticipation around launches, and brand content.
