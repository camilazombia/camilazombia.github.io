# Appointment scheduling

## Summary

I designed the UX/UI experience of an appointment scheduling platform for Bogota's Secretariat of Finance, aiming to bring appointment requests, administration, and tracking together in a single system.

The proposal covered four user roles with different needs and responsibilities: taxpayer, general administrator, branch coordinator, and service agent.

The project included defining flows, features, permissions, scheduling rules, and interfaces, starting from existing processes and the needs identified with the Hacienda team.

## Role and team

I was responsible for the project's UX/UI design process, from understanding the existing operation to structuring flows, defining features, and designing interfaces in Figma.

I worked directly with the Hacienda team, who introduced me to the processes, tools, and scheduling practices they were using at the time.

Based on these sessions, I developed an experience proposal almost from scratch, organizing the different user roles, their responsibilities, and the interactions needed to manage the service.

My contribution focused on design and functional definition from a UX/UI perspective, rather than the platform's technical development.

## Context and challenge

The scheduling process relied on different tools and separate activities.

Opening appointment schedules required sending Excel files to a provider, the request form had usability difficulties, and appointment lookup through Teams did not provide all the information needed for tracking.

The main challenge was to transform these processes into an integrated digital experience where each user could perform their tasks clearly and according to their responsibilities.

The platform needed to address both taxpayers requesting appointments and the teams responsible for configuring, coordinating, and delivering the service.

## Design process

### 1. Understanding the existing process

The project began with meetings with the Hacienda team to review how scheduling worked, which tools they used, and how responsibilities were distributed.

These sessions helped clarify the service journey, from configuring schedules to handling appointments.

### 2. Defining user roles and features

Based on existing processes and the team's needs, I structured the platform's four user roles:

- **Taxpayer:** requests and schedules appointments through a form.
- **General administrator:** manages global system configuration, scheduling rules, and email options.
- **Branch coordinator:** manages schedules and reviews operations for their branch.
- **Service agent:** checks upcoming appointments, tracks them, and accesses service history.

This distinction helped organize features and define the information and actions available to each role.

### 3. Designing flows and rules

I developed the platform's navigation flows and behavior rules, considering the conditions needed to request, configure, and manage appointments.

A central component was the design of dynamic schedules, where adding time slots needed to account for the necessary adjustments to the rest of the schedule.

Request rules and limits were also defined to incorporate these conditions directly into the scheduling experience.

### 4. Interface design

With flows and features defined, I developed the interfaces in Figma, addressing the specific needs of each user role.

The proposal aimed to maintain a consistent experience between the taxpayer form and the management tools used by internal teams.

## Key design decisions

- **Role-based experience:** distinguish features and permissions according to each user's responsibilities.
- **Simplified scheduling:** organize the form to make appointment requests easier for taxpayers.
- **Centralized administration:** bring general configuration and system rules together under a single administrator.
- **Branch management:** allow each coordinator to work with schedules belonging to their branch.
- **Dynamic schedules:** define how time slots behave and relate to availability.
- **Appointment tracking:** give agents access to upcoming appointments, tracking information, and history.
- **Interface consistency:** connect the different processes through consistent navigation and interaction patterns.

## Solution

A UX/UI proposal was developed for a scheduling platform with four distinct experiences.

### Taxpayer

An appointment request flow designed to make it easier to select available options and complete the scheduling process.

### General administrator

A centralized management space for configuring general service conditions, scheduling rules, and email options.

### Branch coordinator

A dashboard focused on each branch's operational administration, with tools to view and manage schedules, including dynamic time slot configuration.

### Service agent

An experience focused on service delivery and tracking, allowing agents to check upcoming assigned appointments, review their information, and access history.

The proposal brought these journeys together within a single design system, accounting for the interactions and states needed by each role.

## Outcomes and learnings

The project structured a comprehensive scheduling proposal, transforming processes that previously relied on separate tools into connected flows and features within a single experience.

One of the main contributions was defining the system's organization, user roles, permissions, and interaction rules from a UX/UI perspective, based on the operational needs shared by the Hacienda team.

The experience strengthened my ability to approach complex digital products, understand institutional processes, and translate functional needs into clear, organized interfaces.

It also reinforced the importance of designing the complete behavior of a service and the relationships between its users, alongside the screens.

## Next steps

As a next step, it would be valuable to conduct usability testing with taxpayers, coordinators, and agents to evaluate their understanding of the flows and ease of completing tasks.

It would also be important to validate operational scenarios for dynamic schedules and review permission behavior in specific situations.

These evaluations would help identify opportunities for improvement before continuing with further product iterations.

## Closing

This project allowed me to take responsibility for the UX/UI design of a platform with multiple user roles, rules, and operational needs, working directly with the team responsible for the service.

The main learning was understanding how to transform complex institutional processes into a structured digital experience where each user can find the tools needed to fulfill their role.
